Control and optimization theory for cyber-physical and autonomous systems

We develop innovative control and optimization approaches for cyber-physical systems — from traffic, transport and automotive systems, to robotics, biological and healthcare systems, and wildfire management. We employ AI-based learning and computation that let those controllers work on real-world. Our work is grounded on fundamental principles of control theory, optimization and AI, and carries through to the real-world deployment.


The mision of the laboratory is research and teaching in the field of automatic control, optimization and robotics. The laboratory has a good research record in the theory, computation and real-world applications of control systems technology and optimization to cyber-physical systems.

Research

Constrained control of interconnected systems

How can many coupled systems be controlled within their limits with decentralized control?

We employ set invariance methods (including periodic invariance and zonotopes) for decentralized constrained control. Our interpolating control approach computes invariant sets offline and solves an LP online to find the control law. A scalable alternative to MPC, with good theoretical properties and guarantees.

Scialanga, Olaru, Ampountolas, Int. J. Control, 2023.

Traffic management & connected/automated vehicles

How should traffic signals and connected vehicles be managed across large-scale road networks?

We design traffic management schemes for large-scale multicommodity road networks that include connected and automated vehicles. This includes advanced model predictive control of traffic signals, perimeter flow control and routing using the store-and-forward modelling paradigm (TUC strategy) and the link transmission model with queue dynamics.

Wei, et al., IEEE T-ITS, 2025; Jusoh, Ampountolas, CEP, 2024; De Souza, et al., IEEE T-ITS, 2022; Ampountolas, Zheng, Geroliminis, TR-Part-B, 2013 and 2017; Ampountolas, et al., TR-Part-C, 2009 and 2010; Diakaki, Papageorgiou, Ampountolas, CEP, 2002

Advanced driver assistance systems in the real world

How do commercial driver assistance systems actually behave in platoons, and what does that do to traffic flow; energy and fuel consumption; and string stability?

We assess commercial systems such as adaptive cruise control using empirical data. In test campaigns at Vicolungo in Italy and AstaZero in Sweden, platoons of ACC vehicles used more energy the further upstream a vehicle sat, while platoons of human drivers did not.

Apostolakis, Ampountolas, IEEE-T-VT, 2024; Ampountolas, IEEE-T-IV, 2023; Apostolakis, et al., IEEE T-ITS, 2023

Dictate physical laws through learning

Can a neural network comply with the physical laws model and learn their parameters using empirical data?

We build physics-inspired neural networks that efficiently learn model parameters, for adaptive cruise control systems and wildfire spreading models. The optimized models can be then used for real-time prediction.

Vogiatzoglou, et al., CMAME, 2025; Apostolakis, Ampountolas. IEEE-T-VT, 2024

Sensing and spatio-temporal dynamics

Where should sensors go, and what do their records reveal about a city?

We use information-theoretic methods to select and place traffic sensors, and Bayesian non-parametric clustering to find structure in sensor and user-generated spatio-temporal data.

Venkatasubramaniam et al., J. Appl. Stat., 2023

Optimization of mobility & infrastructure

How mobility and infrastructure should be better managed in smart cities?

Innovative motorway tidal-flow lane control and bus-bunching mitigation — control and operations research methods applied to the systems cities actually run.

Ampountolas, Kring, IEEE-T-ITS, 2020; Ampountolas, dos Santos, Carlson, IEEE-T-ITS, 2019
Open-source software

Interpolating Control Toolbox

A free, open-source MATLAB toolbox for constrained control of discrete-time linear systems. It handles time-invariant systems and uncertain time-varying systems, with global or local constraints on states and controls, in centralized or decentralized form.

Equipment

The laboratory is equipped with state-of-the-art equipment including an indoor testbed facility for ground and aerial vehicles, where control and learning methods are tested on real hardware.


Autonomous Vehicles Research Studio

A state-of-the-art research studio for running a multi-vehicle programme with aerial and ground vehicles, alone or in swarms. A ground-station computer, a wireless router and an OptiTrack motion-capture system tie the vehicles together, and the software is built on QUARC for Simulink.

People

Konstantinos Ampountolas

Professor of Automatic Control · Director, Automatic Control & Autonomous Systems Laboratory

The lab is led by Prof. Konstantinos Ampountolas, with doctoral and postdoctoral researchers working across control, optimization and learning.

Prof. Ampountolas holds a PhD degree in Control Engineering (Technical University of Crete, 2009); He has been in the Department of Mechanical Enginnering at the University of Thessaly since 2019; previously he was Senior Lecturer (Associate Professor) at the James Watt School of Engineering, University of Glasgow (2013–2019), Research Fellow at EPFL (2012–2013), visiting scholar at University of California, Berkeley (2011), and visiting professor at Technion, Chalmers, and the Federal University of Santa Catarina. Invited talks at UC Berkeley, ETH Zürich, UCLA, UCL, KTH, The University of Tokyo and more.

His research interests include automatic control and optimization theory with emphasis on applications to cyber-physical systems (e.g. traffic, transport, automotive systems, complex networks, etc.).
